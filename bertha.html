Bertha

Bertha

by Perry Hotchkiss

She awoke to the light wind fluttering the leaves of the trees pleasantly. The sweet twittering of her small and gentle bird friends all around nearby, was assurance that the day would be good. She stretched languidly, noticing a slight bit of crispness in the air.

Days of warmth, showers, and blooming wildflowers, would soon change to occasional gusts of briskness. The quiet of her forest home was now and then disturbed by the chattering of a stellar jay, harassing some small animal like a squirrel, chipmunk, or vole. They were pleasing to the eye and larger than many birds, but grating on the ears and pesky by nature. Their habit of raiding other bird's nests, and generally causing mischief, was irritating.

No other family member was anywhere around as she took the trail to the close-by stream. A bit further up, a family of red foxes were kneeling on a rocky shelf getting a morning drink. The mother looked on contentedly, as the two kits lapped the clear water. High overhead, a large hawk glided by looking for the movement of his morning meal. It's eyesight was keen and constantly scanning for furtive action to and from a variety of burrows. The obvious and hidden forest dens were the home of many kind of animals seeking shelter from hunters and the weather. Some had dug their own homes, and others had claimed those abandoned by previous occupants after a season. A moose cow and her calf, could clearly be seen at the bend of the stream grazing on water plants in the shallows.

Sunbeams pierced some fluffy clouds, and added accent to forest activities below in the valley of the stream. Who would imagine what the day would bring? The gobbling of wild turkeys could be clearly heard in the nearby forest. The noise that their calls made had to attract all kinds of hunters. It was amazing so many roamed these hills and mountain sides. The slow-moving birds flew poorly, and proved to be a seldom, but tasty treat.

Across the ocean from the Canadian coastline, a friendly man lived in a semi-tropical rain forest. His modest, but attractive hut, was nestled protectively in an ohia/hapu'u acreage with minimal utilities or unnecessary improvements. He enjoyed doing some gardening, but, being retired, his days were those of leisure, smoking his pipe and having a coffee while listening to news of the world on his radio. His porch, (or lanai in Hawaiian), was where he spent much of his time. From his bench there, he could not see another structure of any kind. That was the way he liked it. In an effort to simplify his life, he was selective about how much he interacted with other people. Those he did choose to see in person or talk with on the telephone, were friends. He usually didn't meet people accidentally. He shared his home with a colorful but ornery parrot named Samuel. They coexisted, and Denis liked the parrot more than the bird liked him, (the bird preferred female humans it seemed), but to have a companion with feathered green legs, was a bonus. More friendly birds of the wild type, flocked to the bird feeder in front of the porch. There were doves, cardinals, canary finches, apapane/ (Hawaiian song birds), elepaios, sparrows, mynas, and thrushes. A type of Chinese pheasant called a kalish, also lived in the forest. Denis, (being a bird whisperer of sorts), befriended these large birds by feeding them daily and talking to them. Those that visited frequently, ranged from none to ten or more. A couple of the older ones actually came on his lanai, and pecked on his sliding glass door, to get his attention to be fed! His cat made an effort to hunt some of the younger kalish. This was a big mistake. The older kalish chased the bold, older cat around the yard pecking it unmercifully, removing hunks of fur, until she ran screaming into the trees. It took days for her to return, then a "truce" was called from there on. Animal interactions and politics.

Denis had an open invitation to visit his son and daughter in law, who lived on acreage on the outskirts of Vancouver, and had a plant nursery business. He had only visited twice, since moving from Canada to Hawaii's Big Island permanently. Getting older, he felt it was time to visit while he was still in fairly good condition, and could do some hiking in the forest where he grew up, and enjoyed the experience. He called his son Cletus, to discuss a one week stay and visit. The timing was right before the weather turned too cold, and arrangements were made for airline tickets, and time and place for pickup. Getting kind of excited, Denis remembered "way back when," being a drummer in a rock and roll band that played gigs from Southern California to Alaska. Oh well, long ago and far away. His memory was good, and he discovered books now were the way that he loved to travel, at leisure. He prepared for one more trip and got three pounds of Macadamia nuts, and some Hawaiian Host chocolates and cookies, and a few pounds of "Kona" coffee.

His friend Peter dropped him off at Hilo Airport in exactly eight days at 1 PM. They had plans for lunch at "Ponds" restaurant beforehand. Denis's friend and neighbor Mike, agreed to feed the cat and birds while he was gone. A break in his "pickle ball" playing may be appreciated too. He remembered to include some warm clothing and shoes, and was getting kind of excited for an older guy. A Hawaii friend, a writer named Leroy, had spent four years living in the Pacific Northwest before moving to the Islands. He asked Denis to always be aware of his surroundings; that snakes, bears, moose, and mountain lions lived there in numbers.

A sunny day greeted Denis as Pete picked him up at his driveway for the trip to lunch and the airport. Laughter and good times were had along with great food overlooking the pond. When they arrived curbside, Peter got out to help unload Denis' two medium bags. He then presented him with an open-ended men's maile lei, and said, "Remember to return my friend." Then he left, and Denis headed for the gate and baggage check. Both flights were smooth, and the in-flight movie was "Valerian and the City of a thousand Planets". Boy that one was a corker. It was like the "bar scene" in "Star Wars", but lasting over two hours!

It was a brisk forty eight degrees at Vancouver Airport when Denis arrived. He was glad that he wore long pants, socks and boots, a watch cap, long sleeved T shirt under a cable knit sweater and jacket! "Be prepared if possible", was his motto. His family met him at the gate with hugs and welcoming banter. Cletus asked Denis if he wanted to hit an eating establishment before home, and he said. "No, just a lot of coffee and two grilled cheese sandwiches, and I'll be fine." Angie said, "No problem with that, Denis." He had never been to his son's new custom home, and was duely impressed. The large living room and grand fireplace made it feel like a lodge. The tiled floor was a little cold on his feet in socks, and Cletus said, "Dad, check this out." He went to a panel on the wall and turned a rheostat type of dial. Within three minutes the floor tiles were as least 20 degrees warmer! "Wow" he said, "Is your whole home floor heated?" Cletus said "Yes, but we usually only heat it in sections to be environmental. It will be on in the bedrooms and bathrooms for your visit. We haven't had any snow flurries yet, thank goodness." "You both have done a wonderful job with your new home and you deserve it!" Denis said. Angie said, "Ok guys. Come for "Kona" coffee and grilled cheese sandwiches. A big thanks for the wonderful coffee "Dad". She said. I love to smell it still in the opened package." How cool. They sat at the table with a fine view of the fireplace, and Denis thought, "My son did well, Bless His Heart!"

The next morning Denis slept in until 9 AM. He didn't want to leave the cozy bed. After washing up and dressing warmly for breakfast, he met his son in the kitchen. Cletus said, "I'm cooking breakfast. How about "Huevos rancheros"? Denis said, "Sounds great. Do you have any sour cream?" "Sure do." said the cook. "Coffee?" he inquired.

"For sure, Son. It sure is nice here. We are all very fortunate." "You are the lucky one, Dad, to live in the Hawaiian Islands! Your angels must have been watching over you!" Denis said, "Both places have many benefits. I forgot some of what it was like here, and realize now that it feels like home too. It's a conundrum." Cletus said, "Do you like the big bedroom that you have here with the on-suite bathroom with bathtub complete with Jacuzzi jets?" "Who wouldn't, Cletus?" Denis asked. "Well, Angie and I have talked this over these last few months, and that room is yours to use the rest of your life as much as you want! You are always welcome, Dad." "That's very generous, son, and I will ponder your offer carefully." he said.

Later, Denis asked Cletus, "May I borrow a car from you guys for the day? I'd like to drive up the five miles to the National Park, and go for a hike." "Sure Dad, Cletus said, Here are the keys to my new Jeep Rubicon. Walking stick and bear spray are in the far back space." "Great, Denis said, I'll get ready." He then fired up the off-road style Jeep, and soon hit the highway that went toward the National Park. When he arrived at the parking lot accessing 3 trail heads, he chose the one with the trail along the Salmon River. He had been out it before and remembered the giant trees and the natural beauty of the whole area. Denis passed two couples coming out, as he reached the second mile in, and said, "Aloha" without thinking, and all four smiled and looked at him funnily, and continued on their way out. At the four mile mark, he decided to have lunch on a big rock by the river, before heading back. The hiking made him pretty hungry.

Nearby, he noticed a slapping sound, and thought "Bear!", and grabbed his bear spray. Denis stood up and looked around. The sound was coming from a small alcove in the river, one hundred feet away. He loaded up his gear, and went to investigate! When he rounded the curve in the trail, he saw the form of a shapely woman standing in the river shallows, and batting salmon to the bank effortlessly! He said, "Nice trick," as he approached. She turned suddenly, revealing a quite beautiful face with a smile, but said nothing. Denis said, "I'm Denis!" She pointed at herself and said, "Bertha!" He then noticed that she was at least 6 and ½ feet tall, weighed a curvy 250 pounds, and was closing the distance too him rapidly! This was when he noticed that she was covered with a fine coat of downy tan hair, as she pulled him to her! She had an odd but not unpleasant smell, and he wasn't yet alarmed, until he felt a wall of hair up against him from behind. He turned a little and looked up at two male Sasquatches each 8 feet tall and easily 500 pounds, very hairy and pungent! "Wait!" he said lamely. The bigger one smiled, picked him up, pack and all, and tucked him under his arm and grunted. All three, with an amazed Denis in tow, strode away quickly into the deep woods and disappeared. Bertha had a present!

Om.

— The End —
