Gus the Great

Gus the Great

by Perry Hotchkiss

There was a lighthouse keeper on an island called La Jument three miles off the coastline of Brittany, France. He had been on site almost the entire last two years. Braving some of the most extreme weather patterns ever recorded, his isolation was about to come to an end with the completion of his two year contract. In 1989, the waves from the North Sea began to build even more than before. Normally, the lighthouse, solidly built on a rocky islet, felt sound and secure. Watertight to the top and built very well of stone, it felt impregnable.

Access was by boat only in periods of relative ocean calm, which were intermittent. His hobbies of Biological and Oceanographic photography, has morphed into a correspondence course to finish his Biology degree. He had just completed his study and tests and received a Bachelor of Science in Biology from the Sorbonne. Lately, his supplies had to be dropped at the top by helicopter. His removal may have to be in a similar fashion. The waves were increasing in size and strength. One should remember, that farther south at Nazare in Portugal, the world's largest waves have been surfed! The growing strength of "normal waves", added by the appearance of gigantic "rogue waves", caused by undersea earthquakes, landslides, or extreme storms, started to worry him.

With his Sat phone for emergencies, and the internet to stay in touch with his family on the mainland of France and on the east coast of the USA, he had felt ok until these last waves submerged the lighthouse over two thirds of the way to the top. He was done finally, and would be taken out by helicopter. He had things to do and people to see. He returned to the mainland, back to his family's farm outside of Lyon in central-south France. The Malgorn family had been there for over eighty years. Their dairy cattle produced quality milk to make cheese. It was then stored carefully below ground to age for later sale. After his close call recorded by a news helicopter, he had welcomed the short helo trip to shore with his gear, and passed the baton to another "worthy". He was welcomed back after a long period by his brother Rob, Mom Inez, and their current workers. His small cottage, east of the main house, barns, and out buildings, gave him the quiet that he needed to plan the next step of his life. He put out some feelers in the life science community concerning projects or expeditions requiring a person familiar with Biology and skilled at photography. After a relaxing three months helping at the farm, visiting Lyon and Marseilles, and some of the smaller villages, he heard back on two possibilities. The first was for a marine biologist/photographer for Ireland and Scotland. The second choice was to be part of a Biology/Botany expedition to the Kamchatka Peninsula in Russia opposite Alaska. It required a second photographer and Biologist for a trip that could run from four to eight months. He spoke English, French, and a little Spanish, so this could be the one for him. He made a toll-free call to make further inquiries. The trip was by train from Istanbul to Yakutsk, and by float plane to their destination/study base.

The area was purported to be pristine but rough. Survival there was not guaranteed. The presence of the largest brown bears on the planet, and their probable curiosity piquing their interest in humans, not to mention their probable hunger, made normal trips outdoors only to be essential with a competent armed escort. He was glad that he brought his 1917 Colt semi-auto for eventualities. Of the ten members of the expedition and the six at the center, all were armed to the teeth. Oh Boy!, Theo said to himself, as he checked out the massive logs of their headquarters. It also had cameras, an inside water source, and an overall bear-proof construction. They were there to study and photograph the wildlife, collar viable specimens, and observe the animal and plant life remotely and by using telephoto lenses. The electric fences around the place were strong, but not enough to dissuade a mad and/or amped giant, wild bear! They were pretty much only acting as alarms, not deterrents. The animals of all types were big and powerful in Kamchatka.

A young female bear, (code name Lisa), was darted and fitted with a collar to track her movements. She had recently given birth, and without a den, was trying to protect her single cub against various interested predators. The group had a monitor in her area nearby, and loud fighting sounds were coming from there. Four armed men went to investigate. As we arrived, she had placed herself in front of her cub, and was taking deadly swipes from a gigantic male intent on eating the cub, and maybe her! It was too much, and three men with fifty caliber hunting rifles, each shot the large attacker twice, and it took a total of six solid hits to put him down and out! She was irreparably injured and died within minutes. The cub was terrified but uninjured, unbelievably. Theo wrapped him in his jacket, and they returned to the headquarters where he put him in a big dog carrier by his bed, with some old clothes of his and some blankets.

He only weighed 30 pounds and was mostly docile, seeming to know that he had been saved. He had still been nursing, and Theo and a Ukrainian nurse, Edna, put together a formula in a bottle for him. He focused on it, and it may have saved his life. All knew that he had to be taken from the area when their studies were complete, or he would die there. Theo adopted him and they bonded. He was named Gus, and responded when called. Mostly, he watched everyone, and they played with him like he was another "camp dog!" Gus and Mel the camp dog became friends and Gus had taken on the dog's behavior. They laid by the fire together. It was a little odd, but friends can come in all shapes and sizes. At the age of five months, he was crated, and traveled by air with the crew to the French countryside near Lyon. Soon they were home at the farm.

Theo's brother Rob, had built a long sturdy pen for Gus, so if needed, he could be contained. He listened well to Theo, and behaved like the dog, Fanny, as he was raised on Malgorn Farm. The family eventually welcomed Gus, and no tragedies occurred. Gus often slept in his Dad's cabin by his bed. He especially liked to have his ears rubbed, and under his chin. Theo had contacted famous "bear wrangler" Doug Seuss, whose trained bears Tank and Bart appeared in various movies and commercials. He had also raised his bears from cubs with dog companions. Fanny, the family's sheep dog, was still under a year old, and matured with Gus. They often slept together by the fireplace; (with a screen, of course.) Doug explained that he used "Love training", and dealt with the Bears like they were big dogs. He said, "I've seen wild polar bears of huge size, playing in the snow with dogs without injury to either party. With bears, you may speak with them strongly, but don't berate them. They are as smart or smarter than most dogs, and are sensitive! Doug asked Theo about Gus' age and weight? He said, Kamchatka brown bears are some of the largest on Earth. My grizzlies are from North America. I had another one too, but he was untrainable and was returned to the wild directly, and disappeared into the north woods of British Colombia. Doug mentioned, There may probably be room for another trained bear in movies and commercials in a couple of years when Bart retires. If that is of interest to you, keep doing what you are doing. Gus is a fine animal. If he sleeps by your bed and loves you, I can tell you that's the best protection." Gus was fed "reject cheese", Purina Hi pro dog food with milk, lots of veggies and root crops, and fruit. Papayas and watermelons pleased him, and occasionally, he was given a little beer now and again.

As months went by, Gus's pen became higher and curved in at the top to comply with French "wild animal guidelines." Also, Rob had a rock dome build for Gus at the south end of his enclosure. It had a wide front entrance, was 20 feet in diameter, and a dry place for him to go in case of inclement weather or to sleep. A bear den. He came when Theo called him, and fitted with a big special harness/leash, could be walked around the farm, but not near the cattle. (They got nervous if they saw him, and it upset their milking schedule.) Family and a few friends could be in the house with him, but Theo was careful. At one year, Gus was approximately 800 lbs. And half grown. Because of his good diet, he was gaining weight regularly. He loved his Dad, and would lick on him now and then. Theo was okay with the expression of affection. It was preferable to others.

Gus was gentle around dogs and people. He seemed to know how soft and fragile they were. Theo had him responsive to fifteen vocal commands, and he was learning another ten! Sometimes, when he slept in the living room, he would sleep in the "feet in the air" position, like some dogs do. With Gus, it was a little disturbing, because it showed his big claws and also his ginormous teeth if his lips fell back! Every three months, the French branch of the SPCA came to check on him and his treatment. Om.

The other night around midnight, we all heard an explosion at our outer gate on the highway, followed by a horrendous roar from Gus!! Mom called 911 as we heard drunken screeching saying "We've come for the damn bear!" Ray had grabbed his shotgun and pulled his truck outside our inner gate crosswise blocking the access. I then joined him with my 45, and Mom stayed inside. The idiots, about four in number, and drunk and armed, yelled, "Give us the bear. We'll kill the bastard, and go home. Rob said, "If you throw all your firearms over either fence a ways and just have knives, I'll let Gus out to come say hello!" "Like holy hell!" the leader shouted as two Gendarme cars arrived, and blocked their exit! Essentially, the Gendarme Sgt. Said, "You drunken louts drop your weapons, and lay face down in the dirt road there, and the six of us police won't open fire!" Some drunken swearing and cussin' occurred, but then a somewhat sensible soul spoke up, He loudly proclaimed, "We and others don't like them, and that bear in there, just so you know!" They then dropped their pistols and rifles, and knives, and lay face-down in the dusty road. They were roughly man-handled into a paddy wagon, and rushed to the nearby precinct. The Sgt. Had a work with Theo and Rob and said, "It's probably just jealousy and idiocy, but this might not be the end of it". Theo said, "We don't like them either, and we and the bear will be ready!" The gendarme Sgt. Grinned, I haven't seen Gus since I brought the family six months ago to see him and get some of that good cheese from your Mom. Is he about the same?" Rob replied, "He's gained at least thirty pounds since then, but we can still walk him on his leash!" "My Lord!" said the Sgt. and walked to his waiting patrol car.

Another big hit with Gus, was his giant eight foot by twenty foot by four foot deep water tank of steel. It held at least two thousand gallons, and was filled like two cattle tanks of the same size, from the windmill pulling water from the well. He usually drank his fill from it first, then rolled over the rim with a big splash. Mom liked how often all she could see was his snout! He never leapt out from a submerged position, but surfaced slowly like a submarine. He spent quite some time in it on a hot day, their Gus did. When he did emerge, he would shake all over like a giant dog, and one should stay way back. Then he would go to his grassy spot and roll around there too, and maybe chase his ball around in good fun. Eventually, when he reached three years old and about fifteen hundred pounds, Theo and he were offered two thousand five hundred francs to appear in a PBS one hour program. In it they interviewed the whole family with Gus sitting next to Theo on a leash; (Only a ten foot wood table separated the film and reporter crew from Gus and his family. The family was relaxed, whereas the PBS crew were always toweling themselves down, even though the AC was run on high. Theo was asked "where he found Gus, and his weight and age at that time?" Somewhat sadly, Theo said, "I rescued the cub, after his mother was attacked and killed defending herself and her cub from a much larger male bear, who was then killed with difficulty. Gus was less than 3 months then and weighed about 30 pounds." The reporter asked, "You mentioned the attacking bear was killed with difficulty. What does that mean?" Theo stated, "As he charged us, he had to be shot three times each by our guides with fifty caliber high-powered rifles. We gathered up the cub and returned to our fort-like headquarters, prior to the arrival of other predators for site cleanup. Theo said, I then adopted Gus and nursed him to more health with the assistance of many of the crew, besides their regular jobs.

Upon return to France, my brother Rob and friends had already constructed an all-weather pen near the house for Gus. When not sleeping by my bed, he would live in a custom "Bear Hut" made for him inside of his pen. Until he neared one thousand pounds and was damaging the wood floors from his weight, he was part of the family and slept with our sheepdog, Fanny, who helped a lot in raising him." they addressed Rob and Theo's Mom, and asked, "weren't you concerned that he might kill or injure your dog or yourselves, if not only by accident?" Rob said, "Unlike most humans, Gus seems to think before acting. There's a lesson there to humankind. Of course, he does not harm any of us. He loves us as family, but licks our Mother a little more than she would like, requiring more outfit changes. So far, he hasn't learned to do laundry!" Everyone laughed lightly.

Gus drooled a bit, and seemed sleepy. "I believe that Gus needs to go to his pen and bed. Theo said. Please stay seated until he's out of the house, thank you." he said. When the door closed they all went to watch the young man and his large bear walking side by side to the pen. Gus waited patiently, the door opened and he and Theo entered. Theo removed Gus' harness, hugged his head and patted his back. He later said that he told Gus, "Good Bear!" Nora, the PBS narrator, said, "He's obviously magnificent. I'll remember this for the rest of my life!" "I can tell that he loves all three of you by the way that he regards you. I can only imagine the challenge, but you are all so fortunate to befriend such a big and imposing creature." "We do feel lucky", Inez added. He's earning his keep from shows like this and viewings while in his pen. Perhaps, these interactions, will temper the fear that most humans have for the outside world. I imagine that if we didn't shoot at all the wildlife we see, their attitude toward us would not be as hostile." Desmond, the cameraman said, "A valid observation. I think it's time to buy some cheese and go." Mom laughed, and said, "follow me please." They went to the office/store.

Life went on pretty regularly, considering they lived around a unique bear. If he became perturbed or upset, any of the family could sing to him, "Little Drummer Boy", and he would relax. It had worked since his cub-hood, and probably would continue to work, God willing. Gus's walks had to be more frequent as he got bigger, and twice a day was the minimum. People on the road would wave at the man inside the fence and tree line, walking his bear. Gus could easily go right through the boundary fence, but so far, he didn't want to. (They were lucky that no neighboring female bears were nearby and in season. If that was the case, even his stout pen could probably not hold him.)

The bear walkers started carrying a bag of plums with them on their walks, as treats for good bear behavior. It worked for not chasing the moped, cat, or a wayward mime. A month later, an idiot pilot in a biplane buzzed them while they were strolling. Gus got up on his rear legs, (which was kind of scary), and jumped up and down trying to reach the plane like King Kong did in New York. Singing to him didn't work that time. After some difficulty, Theo returned the bear to his pen, and called the French FAA. He mentioned "their special, science permit, and that Gus the bear was a tourist industry attraction. He proposed that anywhere within a three mile radius of their farm be a "No fly Zone".

This was agreed to by an officer of the French Dept. of the Interior named Francoise Colbert; (bear?). A photo of Gus being led on a leash on the farm was shown all over France, the European Union, and internationally. Not long after, a hunter wrote "that he would pay 30,000 dollars to hunt the giant bear with his elephant gun on the farm." The family was alarmed. Inez wrote back, "only if first, she and her sons could visit the man's estate and hunt all his relatives and retainers with crossbows." No further hunting inquiries were received. Gus was gradually responding more to word and sound commands from the family. They believed that he tried to learn, to please them; and it did. This was a nice development. (The Famous "Bart the Bear", had a repertoire of 100 vocal commands or sounds!) Gus enjoyed being brushed with a stiff snow brush, and the plastic ice blade was used as a massage tool. Theo got Gus trained to trot slowly next to him as Theo ran alongside. It made a good video for many. Once, Theo stumbled and fell forward, dropping the leash. The frantic bear, obviously worried that his "Dad" was hurt, tried to revive him. Theo lifted his hands up and out, and said, "Gus, please stop!" He did. Smells for bears are a challenge. Their sense of smell is four hundred times stronger than a human's. Therefore if people in the house were preparing three-Cheese pizza, Gus would "talk at them", to not be forgotten and get his too. Once Inez gave him a gallon of vanilla ice cream in a cardboard carton. Gus sat down with it in his lap, got the lid off, licked it out thoroughly, and then ate the carton of course!

Though he likes almost all veggies and root crops like onions and carrots, Gus didn't like Kale, and if in the house while it was being cooked, he made a huffing sound. They quit using it when he was around. Inez found out, not surprisingly, that Gus liked most pies. Usually, he was given pies in the yard because he tended to be a little messy. A funny thing happened when Gus was still a cub. Both he and his dog-sister were cuddled right of the fireplace in the dark. A visiting preacher speaking with Inez raised his voice a bit at her in 'the holy spirit'. The "bear rug" arose, approached him directly, Put his face in the preacher's crotch, nosed him forward forcefully until he went over backward and hit the floor hard! Then Gus went over and sniffed his head thoroughly, while grumbling lightly. The preacher left never to return, thankfully. Good Bear. No one yells at Gus's Mom. Sometimes, when cooking or doing the dishes or other kitchen tasks, Inez felt watched. She looked up and saw a bear sitting in the doorway to the living room carefully and quietly watching her. At first it made her unnerved, but then she studied his face. He was "Moon-eyeing" his Mom with admiration. Later, he would sit next to her chair as she listened to the radio. She rubbed his ears and head, and he would make a rumbling sound kind of low. "Bear purring?" Perhaps. Like most of God's creatures, Gus responded to love and good treatment. He seemed to realize after getting larger, that his soft-skinned relatives required more gentle handling. Good. Now and then, when walking the property with Gus, Thep would come upon some wild flowers. He would lay down and look at the clouds, and the bear would lay down next to him and eat the flowers roots and all! Theo's brother Rob, played the recorder on the house's front steps. Gus was in his pen nearby, and he noticed that the bear would get as close as he could to hear him play the recorder. Gus liked most music, but sensibly, not opera, rap, or whiny country western. He howled when Barry Manilow came on the radio. His tunes made the bear's lips turn back, which wasn't a pleasant sight.

Gus loved strawberries and if you gave them to him, he liked or loved you! He also loved milk on his dry dog food, and 'reject cheese', the more the better. Having Fanny the sheepdog as his companion was a boon. Fanny, bless her heart, was a good dog, but probably not the brightest. She probably thought of Gus as a really big dog, which he sort of was. When he arrived from overseas, he was given two things: a giant, heavy duty, corduroy dog pillow, and a soccer ball. When his pen was up, (a twenty by eighty oval), he would kick and nose his ball around the pen, and often fanny would join in. As he got bigger, his ball became a medium size, then giant medicine ball of some weight. He still could move it along. People watching would say, "Look at the trained bear go!" The family was pleased.

When doing un-wild bear like activities, Gus seemed less scary, and more like the ally he was. One day Brother Rob was walking Gus on the leash. Nearby, Mom was trying to move a grazing Holstein into the milking barn. The cow kicked at her twice very fast, and she fell backward to avoid injury. Gus growled and drug Rob over. He rammed the larger cow with his head and shoulder in it's side knocking it to the ground! Then he went to Mom, and licked her some to reassure her, and continued his walk. He had protected his Mom. At nine months, he weighed between nine hundred and one thousand pounds. Because he had developed a gentle nature, he was reliable, good, and responded to vocal instructions and some hand signals. Theo was contacted by a TV/Commercial Company. They had seen a video of Gus, and were wondering if using him in a commercial was a possibility. They came for an appointment, and Theo and Gus were standing together in his pen. Their idea was to film Gus moving a ball around in an open field. The motto would be "exercise is good for any one!" Theo, Rob, and Mom said "Bring your camera tomorrow, and we'll try it on the south pasture. It has some flowers, but not many." The appointment was at 9AM. The van and crew were parked. The crew got all set up, but were nervous around the huge bear. Theo said, "Don't be afraid, you'll be near your open van, Mom and Rob will be there between Gus and you, and I'll be near, but off camera. If all goes OK, we have treats you and we can give Gus."

They agreed. Gus and Theo moved out in the field. He rolled the medicine ball forward and Gus got excited. He disconnected Gus, and the bear slowly romped up to the ball, nudged it with his shoulder, then his head, then the other shoulder, moving it 100 feet. Theo said, "Good Gus. Come bring your ball!" Gus did and moved it back toward the camera. Frederick, the cameraman, said, "I got all of that in good lighting with Gus enjoying ball play. Perhaps if he had a big soccer-looking ball, we could encourage kids to play soccer." "Maybe later." Mom said. Theo had re-leashed Gus and given him a basket of strawberries. He was eating them fast. Rob said to the crew, "each take a basket, approach slowly, leave them in a pile for Gus, and retreat to your van." They looked nervous, but first the woman, then the two men did so, then Fred photographed Gus eating strawberries! It was a good day. The family received a copy of the recorded tape, and a check for Gus's performance for two thousand francs or seven hundred dollars US. Gus's first acting job went well, and the word got out. The family was so proud of him, that they had two cherry pies, and Gus had a cherry and a pumpkin! He was a happy bear. His "Love-training" and non-meat diet seemed to work with him, and his coat was auburn and gold and kind of glowed!

Since Gus was now too big for the house, one end of his pen was covered by a tarp on the top and one side. Stools, and wood benches were placed there for family visits, hanging out with him, and his drinking and swimming tanks were nearby too. His "Bear House" was at the other end, with room so he could push his ball around it next to the fence. It was a dome ten feet tall, and thirty feet In diameter, made of logs, rocks, and cement. It had a seven foot high, ten foot wide, doorway, and the walls were waterproofed. Rob and Theo put lots of bales of hay in it, plus moss, leaves, and some blankets and his giant pillow. It made a bear nest at the far end, and he could be heard snoring in there from the house a hundred feet away.

His enclosure was large, airy, and solid, and he never once tried to get out. Gus was visited, exercised, and fed frequently daily. People asked to bring him meat, but Mom said, "Thanks anyway, but he's a lac-to-vegetarian". The farm had 3 part-time workers, who milked and handled the cattle, did barn maintenance, cheese production, and visited Gus in his Pen. At the 7 ft/ high gate, big pieces of Reject cheese could be dropped in For him. He often made eye contact with the "giver", and gave a friendly "Whuff" of acknowledgment. Mom and the guys would watch and laugh! Om.

Teachers and school children saw the commercial by the local government on exercise. The farm started to receive requests to visit Gus at his pen, and bring him food. The family answered all queries, and started with three appointments. No candy or flashbulbs please. They would remain and be ever present. There was a wood box on the fence by the gate for "Gus donations", since the appointments so far were free and thirty minutes long. Gus was the only bear known outside of a zoo in France, and maybe bordering countries. A nice wood sign at the gate said: Molgorn Farm and Creamery: Cheese sold. See Gus the Bear by appointments only. Beware of the Bear! This was a disclaimer advised by the French Government. Insurance was a necessity for tourism in general and Gus in specific. It was noted on signs: "Gus is not tame, but usually friendly." It was true. Inez, (his Mom), would sit on a stool outside the gate, and through a one foot by one foot window, would feed her "Bear Boy" individual carrots, beets, and cooked whole potatoes. Watching this was great! He would take each item with his lips only. Over the coming months and years Gus and his family shared with many the example of "Love training" and inter species communication.

Om.

— The End —
